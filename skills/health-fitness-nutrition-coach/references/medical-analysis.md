# Medical analysis

## Symptoms

Establish onset, duration, severity, trajectory, triggers, associated symptoms, relevant history, medicines and supplements, pregnancy when relevant, and risk factors. Ask only questions capable of changing triage or the short differential.

Rank a short differential in two passes: serious causes not to miss, then more likely plausible causes. For each hypothesis, give supporting facts, conflicting or absent facts, uncertainty, and the professional or test that could distinguish it. Use "these elements can be compatible with" rather than a diagnostic declaration.

## Laboratory results and reports

First capture the test or report name, specimen or modality, body area if applicable, date, exact result, unit, laboratory reference interval or report conclusion, symptoms, and reason for testing. Keep original wording available; identify transcription or OCR uncertainty.

For laboratory data:

- use the reporting laboratory's interval and flagging before any generic range;
- consider fasting, recent activity, hydration, infection, menstrual cycle, medicines, supplements, collection conditions, and method;
- explain each measure simply, then analyze related results together and across time;
- a single value rarely confirms or excludes a disease.

For imaging reports, translate technical terms, preserve the radiologist's degree of certainty, compare reports only when modality, area, and dates are clear, and prepare questions for the treating clinician.

For a raw medical image, verify exam type, body area, date, reason, and whether the full study and report exist. Describe only clearly visible features and image limitations. Use "possibly compatible with" or "an abnormality to verify." A displayed image cannot confirm or exclude fracture, tumor, infection, or other disease; prioritize the radiologist's report.

## Required medical response structure

Use every heading below even for a short answer. State uncertainty as low, medium, or high and justify it briefly; do not leave uncertainty merely implied.

### Urgency level

Immediate emergency / prompt consultation / non-urgent appointment / reasonable monitoring / insufficient data.

### Observed facts

Only supplied values, symptoms, dates, and trends.

### Possible interpretation

General meaning and relevant context.

### Hypotheses to examine

For each: compatible evidence; incompatible or missing evidence; uncertainty; verification route.

### Limits

What cannot be concluded and why.

### Next action

One concrete action with a recommended timeframe and escalation criteria.
