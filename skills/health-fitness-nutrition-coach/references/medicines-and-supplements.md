# Medicines and supplements

## Medicines

Verify the exact product and active ingredient, formulation, stated indication, user-reported dose and frequency, allergies, other medicines and supplements, pregnancy or breastfeeding when relevant, and reported adverse effects. Confirm current information in the official label or medicine database for the user's country.

Explain general use, common and serious adverse effects, precautions, contraindications, and recognized interactions. Separate label facts from user-specific inference. Direct a potential interaction or tolerability problem to the prescriber or pharmacist, with urgency determined by symptoms.

Never select a prescription medicine for the user or advise starting, stopping, substituting, or changing its dose. In particular, do not convert a side-effect concern into an abrupt-stop instruction; some medicines require supervised tapering or urgent review.

Do not rank or substitute medicine salts, dosage forms, excipients, routes, or immediate-, delayed-, and extended-release products from absorption data alone. Check the authorized product information and applicable EMA or national bioequivalence guidance, then direct any proposed switch to the pharmacist or prescriber. Different formulations require product-specific evidence: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/investigation-bioequivalence-scientific-guideline

## Peptides and weight-management medicines

Identify the exact molecule, product, intended outcome, and jurisdiction. Separate endogenous peptides, authorized medicines, supplements, and experimental products. Favor sustainable lifestyle measures and avoid unnecessary peptide use, especially when adequate human safety and outcome evidence is missing. "Natural" is a preference, not proof of safety or superiority; never use it to delay indicated care or discourage a prescribed treatment. Verify current product authorization and evidence before answering: permission to compound, an advisory vote, or a trial registration is not marketing authorization or proof of efficacy.

### GLP-1

Distinguish the body's GLP-1 hormone from GLP-1 receptor agonist medicines. Specific products, such as semaglutide marketed as Wegovy, have human trial evidence and FDA and EU authorization for defined weight-management indications. Authorization does not make every GLP-1 product appropriate or risk-free for every user. Check the exact product's current label, eligibility, contraindications, adverse effects, and monitoring needs; refer treatment decisions to the clinician.

Keep nutrition, physical activity, sleep, and sustainable habits foundational. Support weight management without medication when appropriate and consistent with the user's preference, while recognizing that a clinician may recommend medication alongside lifestyle measures. Do not claim that lifestyle-induced endogenous GLP-1 production reproduces a medicine's effects or that taking medication reflects a failure of willpower. Discourage self-medication and unapproved online or "research-use" products; give no initiation, dose, or discontinuation instructions.

Sources: [EMA Wegovy assessment and product information](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/wegovy); [FDA Wegovy prescribing information, 2026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s030lbl.pdf); [FDA concerns about unapproved GLP-1 drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss).

### BPC-157

Advise against BPC-157 for self-directed healing, injury recovery, or general wellness. Animal findings do not establish human benefit, and "heals everything in the body" is unsupported. Human reports and small pilot studies exist, so do not claim there are no human studies; the available evidence is insufficient to establish reliable efficacy or safety. FDA identifies concerns involving immunogenicity, impurities, and inadequate safety information. Explain these limitations and orient injuries or persistent symptoms to established care; provide no sourcing, injection, dosing, or stacking protocol.

Sources: [human intravenous pilot study, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40131143/); [FDA peptide safety concerns](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks).

### Epitalon / Epithalon

Advise against Epitalon for self-directed anti-aging. Human evidence is limited; neither longer life nor slower clinical aging is established. Distinguish Epitalon from Epithalamin and cell-culture findings from human outcomes. Avoid fixed study counts or dismissing evidence by nationality; assess design, replication, and bias. Safety remains insufficiently characterized. Provide no sourcing, dosing, or injection protocol; redirect to [healthy-aging.md](healthy-aging.md).

Sources: [FDA Epitalon evidence assessment, July 2026](https://www.fda.gov/media/193345/download); [FDA peptide safety concerns](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks). Recheck evidence and regulatory status when answering.

## Supplements

Before discussing benefit, define the exact need and whether it rests on a confirmed deficiency, plausible dietary gap, performance goal, or recognized indication. Check medicines, conditions, allergies, pregnancy, breastfeeding, and overlapping ingredients.

Verify evidence, contraindications, interactions, tolerable upper limits where established, product composition, and quality concerns. Prefer food when it adequately addresses the need. Classify the conclusion as:

- benefit reasonably established for this indication;
- possible benefit with limited evidence;
- unnecessary in this context;
- risk likely exceeds benefit;
- professional review required.

Avoid supplement stacks and duplicated ingredients. Recommend biological or clinical confirmation for a suspected deficiency when it would change management. Anses advises professional review, avoidance of overlapping or multiple supplements, and disclosure to a doctor or pharmacist because adverse effects and interactions occur: https://www.anses.fr/fr/content/la-nutrivigilance

Report suspected adverse effects through the applicable national vigilance pathway after urgent care needs are handled.

### Butyrate products

Explain delivery separately from durable microbiome change. Gastro-resistant, extended-release products are intended to release butyrate after the stomach; actual distribution requires product-specific data. Released butyrate can be absorbed and metabolized, especially by intestinal cells and the liver, rather than accumulate as a lasting microbiome reserve. Do not equate a temporary intestinal supply with restoring bacteria or their endogenous production. [Human isotope metabolism study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27510655/).

Biomalife BUTYRATE LP is an extended-release sodium-butyrate supplement, not a probiotic: the manufacturer lists 625 mg sodium butyrate, equivalent to 500 mg butyric acid, per tablet. It supplies butyrate, not Akkermansia or other bacteria. Treat the manufacturer's release claims as product claims, not evidence of clinical superiority. [Manufacturer composition](https://tortoise-saxophone-l7f3.squarespace.com/butyrate-lp).

Do not extrapolate the small inulin/butyrate trial in [nutrition-and-weight.md](nutrition-and-weight.md) into efficacy for this brand, formulation, microbiome restoration, or weight loss. No product-specific trial establishing those outcomes was identified in this check; recheck when answering. Prefer dietary fiber when appropriate and assess a supplement against the actual indication and health context rather than recommend it routinely or reproduce a seller's dosing schedule.

## Formulation and bioavailability

When products contain different forms of the same nutrient or substance, compare the exact forms instead of treating the ingredient name as sufficient. First obtain the full label, intended outcome, serving size, active or elemental amount per serving, other ingredients, and the user's medicines and health context.

Keep these questions separate:

1. Does the form dissolve or become available for absorption?
2. What fraction is absorbed, and under which meal, dose, or formulation conditions?
3. Is it converted to the relevant active form?
4. Does it change a biomarker or tissue exposure?
5. Does it improve a clinically meaningful outcome for this indication?
6. Are tolerability, toxicity, interactions, contamination risk, and cost acceptable?

Never infer clinical superiority from solubility, absorption, a biomarker, or a manufacturer's "maximum absorption" claim alone. Compare equivalent active or elemental doses; the mass of a salt, chelate, ester, or complex is not necessarily the mass of the active nutrient. Account for dose-response, timing with food, competing nutrients, gastrointestinal tolerance, and whether the studied formulation matches the marketed product.

When asked which product to buy, do not make a user-specific choice from absorption alone. Give the conditional formulation comparison, identify the missing intended outcome and full label, and stop at a conditional assessment until they are known. When the outcome is missing, explicitly say there is not enough information to choose a product yet. A purchase recommendation requires evidence that the formulation difference improves an outcome relevant to that user's need, with acceptable safety and cost.

Give two independent conclusions:

- **Formulation assessment:** preferred with comparative evidence / acceptable alternative / less bioavailable but potentially useful / no demonstrated advantage / insufficient data.
- **Clinical-use assessment:** benefit reasonably established / possible benefit with limited evidence / unnecessary in this context / risk likely exceeds benefit / professional review required.

State the evidence endpoint and confidence. For example, evidence of higher blood concentration is not evidence of symptom relief, injury prevention, or disease treatment. When no direct head-to-head or outcome evidence exists, say that no best form can be identified.

### European evidence route

For product searches, use the Europe → Asia → Americas order in [safety-and-triage.md](safety-and-triage.md), while retaining local authorization checks and scientific evidence from any region.

For a product used in the European Union, prioritize:

1. European Commission and EFSA sources for permitted nutrient sources, upper-level assessments, safety opinions, and authorized or rejected health claims;
2. EMA and the user's national medicines regulator for medicines, product information, formulation, and bioequivalence;
3. national food-safety and vigilance authorities, such as Anses in France, for local warnings and adverse-event signals;
4. current systematic reviews and well-designed comparative trials for efficacy and formulation differences, regardless of country of origin.

Do not treat an EU-permitted ingredient source as proof of superior absorption or clinical benefit. Separately check the EU Register of Health Claims and the underlying EFSA opinion; an authorized claim applies only under its stated conditions, while authorization status does not replace an individualized benefit-risk assessment.

European starting points:

- Food supplements and permitted nutrient sources: https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/food-supplements_en
- EU Register of nutrition and health claims: https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en
- EFSA: https://www.efsa.europa.eu/
- EMA bioequivalence guidance: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/investigation-bioequivalence-scientific-guideline
- Anses nutrivigilance: https://www.anses.fr/fr/content/la-nutrivigilance

### Collagen example

Do not classify oral collagen as ineffective merely by claiming that it is not absorbed. Distinguish digestion and absorption of peptides or amino acids from evidence that a specific collagen formulation improves a specified outcome. Evaluate skin, joint, tendon, and protein-intake claims separately; match the studied hydrolysate or peptide profile, dose, duration, comparator, and funding or risk of bias to the marketed product. If independent, high-quality outcome evidence is inconsistent or absent, classify the benefit as possible but limited rather than recommending it by default.
