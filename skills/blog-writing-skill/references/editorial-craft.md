# Editorial profile and composition

## Build a reusable editorial profile

Create a compact **EDITORIAL PROFILE** from inspected site articles, the author's samples and explicit choices. Reuse an existing confirmed profile when it fits the destination, section and writer; update changed preferences instead of repeating the whole analysis.

Record the following in the working brief:

- **Publication and scope:** site, relevant section or author, sample URLs or supplied excerpts, and when they were examined.
- **Reader and purpose:** assumed knowledge, editorial purpose, language and regional usage.
- **Stable voice:** register, characteristic vocabulary, sentence rhythm, narration, humour and degree of technical explanation.
- **Conventions:** headings, citations, formatting and devices the publication uses deliberately.
- **Author choices:** confirmed preferences and departures from the site's habits.
- **Article tone:** the adjustment for this theme, objective and approved angle; do not turn a one-off tone into a rule for all future articles.
- **Status:** observed traits, uncertain inferences and choices awaiting confirmation. Include a brief example showing how a proposed choice affects the prose when that makes the decision clearer.

Present the useful profile choices alongside the angle and outline, rather than adding a separate approval ceremony. The author can correct the inferred voice. Keep confirmed choices available in the conversation; when cross-session reuse is wanted, offer to save the profile to an author-chosen workspace location. Write it only when requested or accepted. Do not add client profiles to the skill's own files or overwrite a profile for a different site, section or writer.

If the author arrives with an approved angle and outline but no established profile, propose only the material missing tone choices. Do not repeat approvals already given or invent observed site characteristics.

## Compose the article

### Choose the opening

Start with the entry point that serves this piece: an actual supplied scene, a clearly fictional illustration, a concrete example, a question the article answers, a meaningful tension or a direct statement of the thesis. Establish why this subject deserves attention without a generic announcement about a changing world. For a practical guide, explain what the reader will learn; for a literary essay, let an image or scene carry the opening when appropriate.

Write or revise the opening after the body if that helps it represent the final argument. A compelling opening must not promise a result or revelation the article cannot deliver.

### Develop each section

Give each section one main contribution and give each paragraph a clear role. Develop an idea through relevant detail, an example, evidence or explanation of how it works. Connect the next idea through its relationship to what came before; headings and stock transition phrases cannot substitute for reasoning.

Use specific nouns and verbs. Explain unfamiliar concepts when the intended reader needs them. Keep nuance that changes the conclusion, and cut qualifications that merely delay it. Choose lists, dialogue, scenes or continuous prose according to the content and profile, not for visual variety alone.

### Address useful objections

For an argumentative article, consider the strongest relevant objection and respond fairly through evidence, interpretation or the author's stated priorities. Distinguish the competing view from the author's response. If the response needs an authorial judgment not yet supplied, ask rather than fabricate their rationale. Do not force a debate into a descriptive article or manufacture opposing voices.

### Finish with purpose

End when the article has fulfilled its promise. Depending on the agreed form, return to the opening with a developed perspective, state an implication, offer a useful next action or leave an earned question. Avoid mechanically repeating every section or attaching a commercial call to action. A separate conclusion or summary remains optional.

### Test the prose

Read consecutive paragraphs as a continuous piece: does each add something, does the reader understand why the argument moves, and does the ending follow from it? Adjust sentence and paragraph rhythm to the target language and voice. Preserve deliberate repetitions or dramatic pauses when they contribute; remove accidental patterns and filler. Check the finished title against the actual promise of the article.
