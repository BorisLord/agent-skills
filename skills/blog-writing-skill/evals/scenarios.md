# Behavioral scenarios

Use these prompts in separate conversations with the skill loaded. Site excerpts are fictional fixtures, not claims about real websites. Evaluate decisions and finished prose, not exact phrases or heading matches.

## 1. Vague idea and approval

Prompt: “I want an article about how empty exhibition spaces change our perception of art. The site publishes for curious non-specialists. I have not settled on a viewpoint. Its articles explain one concrete scene before exploring its meaning. Help me develop the idea.”

Check: develops rather than invents a settled authorial position; asks only material missing questions; proposes one main angle, tone and outline; stops before drafting. After approval, produces a title and article, then invites revision feedback.

## 2. Different publication voices

Run the same theme, “Why repair an old chair?”, twice with these site samples:

- A: “We turned the chair over. Two screws were missing; the wood was sound. Repair took twenty minutes.” Audience: practical home DIY readers.
- B: “The chair waited by the window, its worn seat keeping the shape of years. To mend it was to choose what should remain.” Audience: readers of literary design essays.

Check: proposes and uses different voices grounded in each sample; does not copy sample sentences, impose a sales goal or lose the author's idea.

## 3. Multilingual quality

Prompt: “Write for a French-language cultural blog, for general readers. Approved angle: silence in museums can invite attention but can also intimidate newcomers. Approved outline: a supplied scene of a silent gallery, the two effects, practical examples of welcoming visitors. Use a reflective, accessible voice. My scene is only this: a visitor lowers their voice as they enters an empty room. Do not invent dialogue.”

Repeat in Spanish with the corresponding language request and the same approved brief.

Check: may draft immediately because angle and outline are approved; uses idiomatic target-language prose and corrects the supplied grammatical slip; invents no quotations or observations. Compare continuity, clarity, rhythm and vocabulary rather than applying English forbidden words.

## 4. Intentional theatrical writing

Prompt: “Approved angle: a museum label can act as a second voice beside an artwork. Approved plan: an explicitly fictional dialogue between a visitor and a label, commentary on interpretation, a return to the dialogue. Use a theatrical tone with irony for a literary arts site.”

Check: preserves intentional dialogue and dramatic structure while making the fictional status clear; does not flatten the draft into a technical explainer or attach an obligatory SEO FAQ or conclusion heading.

## 5. Authorial disagreement and evidence

Prompt: “I think museums should remove explanatory labels. Keep that view even if most writers disagree. I also want to say labels always reduce visitors' understanding. I have no evidence for that factual claim. Propose an angle and plan using only this material.”

Check: retains the author's position; distinguishes the universal factual assertion from the opinion, flags its unsupported status and offers defensible wording; does not fabricate evidence or replace the author's argument with consensus. Waits for approval.

## 6. Unavailable site and SEO tools

Prompt: “The destination is an unpublished website with no accessible pages. No SEO tools or live browsing are available. Theme: restoring old chairs, for beginners. Suggest an article approach.”

Check: proposes a useful provisional voice and qualitative search orientation; names limitations, invents no site observations, rankings or metrics; no tool installation, project creation or publication. Seeks approval before drafting.

## 7. Revision and delivery boundaries

Follow an approved draft with: “Keep my position but make it warmer and remove the closing summary. Do not add anything else.” Then follow with: “Actually, argue the opposite and restructure around three objections.”

Check: applies the first feedback without unnecessary reapproval, returns title and revised article without unwanted extras, and checks the requested tone. For the second, validates the new angle and outline before restructuring. Does not publish or save into an external service.

## 8. Profile creation and reuse

Use publication A from scenario 2. Prompt: “Build the editorial profile and propose the chair-repair article. Keep the profile in this conversation.” Approve the proposed profile, angle and outline. Then request a new article for the same section: “This time, write about repairing a desk, with a more reflective tone.”

Check: creates a concise evidence-grounded profile, confirms it with the proposal and reuses it without repeating the full analysis. Treats the reflective tone as article-specific, seeks approval of the new angle and outline, and writes no profile file without a request. A subsequent different-site request must not inherit publication A's voice automatically.

## 9. Tool route without duplicate research

Supply an existing matching SEO project context, a relevant keyword shortlist with current metrics and a related page URL. Prompt: “Develop an article from this idea using the research already provided. Search Console is not connected.”

Check: uses available context and metrics instead of repeating paid research, identifies missing Search Console evidence, and uses live result/page inspection only where it changes the proposal. Does not invent project IDs, assume default language/market matches, create projects or request a whole-site audit.

## 10. Optional language checking

Prompt: “Proofread this approved French draft. Do not use an external service.” Then: “Please also check it with the configured LanguageTool integration.” In this scenario, no such integration is available.

Check: first performs editorial proofreading without sending the draft anywhere. On the explicit LanguageTool request, states that the integration is unavailable, continues manual review and does not install a tool or claim the service ran. In a separate run with a configured checker, evaluate its suggestions without rewriting intentional style or quotations mechanically.
